Broken Spring Repair

A broken spring changes how the entire door carries its weight.

The opener is not designed to replace the spring’s counterbalance. If the door is heavy, crooked, or will not open, stop operating it and have the complete system inspected.

Signs of a Broken Spring

A loud bang, a visible gap in a torsion spring, a door that feels unusually heavy, loose cables, or an opener that strains without lifting the door.

Matched Spring Pairs

On a two-spring system, LIFTX generally replaces the pair because both springs have normally completed the same number of operating cycles.

Correct Sizing & Cycle Options

Spring selection is based on the actual door system. Standard and higher-cycle options are explained when they fit the application.

Inspect supporting hardware

Set tension and balance

The replacement springs are adjusted so the door carries and moves through its travel correctly.

Test manual operation

The door is moved and balanced manually before the opener is treated as the final test.

Test the complete system

Operator travel, force, controls, and safety devices are checked after the door is properly balanced.

Do not force the door: Operating a garage door with a broken spring can damage the opener, cables, sections, or hardware and can create a safety hazard.

Door Balance & Opener Strain

Should a garage door feel heavy when disconnected from the opener?

No. A properly counterbalanced garage door should be manageable by hand and move smoothly through its travel. It may not feel completely weightless, and exact balance varies by system, but it should not feel excessively heavy, fall rapidly, or depend on the opener to carry the door’s weight.

Top-section or opener bracket movement

If the opener bracket, reinforcement, or backing begins pulling away from the top section, stop operating the door. The door may be too heavy, out of balance, improperly reinforced, or experiencing more than one problem.

Opener strain

Grinding, slowing, stopping, reversing, rail movement, or a trolley that struggles to move the door can indicate a balance problem rather than an opener problem alone.

Manual balance behavior

When the system is safe to test, the door should move without excessive force and should not rapidly fall or shoot upward. A door that will not remain reasonably controlled needs inspection.

What LIFTX checks

Spring sizing and tension, cables, drums, bearings, track and roller friction, section condition, reinforcement, opener attachment, travel, and force settings are evaluated together.

Do not disconnect an unsafe door: If a spring appears broken, cables are loose, the door is crooked, or the door is stuck, leave it in place and call for service.

Broken Spring Questions

Straight answers before service.

How can I tell if the spring is broken?

Common signs include a visible gap in a torsion spring, a loud bang from the garage, a door that will not lift, or a door that suddenly feels much heavier than normal.

Can I open the door with a broken spring?

No. The spring provides the counterbalance that makes the door manageable. Using the opener or forcing the door can create additional damage and a safety hazard.

Should both springs be replaced?

On a two-spring system, LIFTX generally replaces both. They have usually completed the same number of cycles, and replacing only one can leave an uneven or short-lived repair.

How long does spring replacement take?

Many standard residential spring repairs can be completed in one service visit when the correctly sized components are available. The exact time depends on the door and related conditions.

Are higher-cycle springs available?

Yes. LIFTX can explain standard and higher-cycle options when the door, available space, and spring configuration support them.

What causes garage door springs to break?

Normal cycle fatigue is the most common cause. Corrosion, incorrect sizing, poor balance, and other system conditions can shorten spring life.
